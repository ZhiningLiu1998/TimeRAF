\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate \method{} on long-term and short-term forecasting over 16 real-world
benchmarks and 13 base forecasting models. Section~\ref{sec:exp-main} reports the
main results and Section~\ref{sec:exp-analysis} analyses which correction each
deployment ends up using; full configurations are in Appendix~\ref{app:search}.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
For long-term forecasting we use seven standard benchmarks at horizons
$\{96,192,336,720\}$: the four ETT subsets, Weather, Electricity, and
Traffic~\citep{wang2024lib}. For short-term forecasting we use the PEMS traffic
networks PEMS03/04/07/08~\citep{Chen2001FreewayPM} at $\{6,12,24\}$ and the EPF
electricity markets NP, PJM, BE, FR, and DE~\citep{lago2021epf} at horizon 24.
One dataset--backbone--horizon combination is one \emph{setting}, giving 585 in
total: 364 long-term, 156 PEMS, and 65 EPF (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:dataset-scope}).

\paragraph{Base models and setup.}
We correct 13 forecasters implemented in \texttt{TSLib}~\citep{wang2024lib} with
their official configurations, L2 loss, and seed 2021: \timexer{}, \timemixer{},
\pattn{}, \itransformer{}, \timesnet{}, \patchtst{}, \dlinear{}, \frets{},
\fedformer{}, \nonstat{}, \lightts{}, \informer{}, and \autoformer{}
\citep{wang2024timexer,wang2024timemixer,tan2024pattn,liu2024itransformer,
wu2023timesnet,patchtst,dlinear,yi2024frets,zhou2022fedformer,
Liu2022NonstationaryTR,lightts,zhou2021informer,wu2021autoformer}. A backbone is
trained once and never modified;
validation predictions build memory, validation labels lock one correction per
setting, and that correction is applied once to test, so test labels never enter
selection (Appendix~\ref{app:causality}). Every compared system additionally
receives byte-identical base forecasts under one validation split, memory boundary,
and scoring rule, with all grids fixed in advance
(Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval-baselines}), so accuracy differences can only come
from what is retrieved. Long-term and EPF settings report MSE and MAE in normalized
space and PEMS reports inverse-scaled MAE, RMSE, and MAPE; a \emph{strict win} is a
setting in which every metric it reports decreases. Because base errors span an
order of magnitude, we report medians of paired gains alongside means.

\paragraph{Compared systems.}
Equation~\ref{eq:dial} lets us compare retrieval objects rather than
implementations. \analogknn{} pins it at $\beta=1$, injecting from the same
neighbourhood and descriptor the endpoint-aligned futures that input-retrieval
systems emit, and \residualknn{} pins it at $\beta=0$.
\raft{}~\citep{han2025raft} and \saraf{}~\citep{zhou2026saraf} contribute two
published operators on the same fixed forecasts, while \method{} chooses $\alpha$,
$\beta$, and the horizon profile per setting on validation.

\paragraph{Long-term forecasting.}
In Table~\ref{tab:main-long-term}, \method{} attains the lowest average MSE and
MAE, 0.380/0.376 against 0.450/0.413 uncorrected, and is best on both metrics for
six of the seven datasets; only on Traffic is \residualknn{} lower on MSE. The two
pinned objects disagree rather than one dominating: retrieving futures averages
0.392/0.383 against 0.418/0.398 for retrieving errors, and neither leads a dataset
once the object is chosen per setting. That choice is what recovers both, with median
paired reductions of 4.17\% MSE and 2.62\% MAE against 2.27\%/0.90\% for \analogknn{}
and 2.41\%/1.20\% for \residualknn{}.

\paragraph{Short-term forecasting.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-short-term} shows a wider margin on shorter horizons, where
\method{} is best on both reported metrics for all nine datasets. On PEMS it
averages 19.407 MAE and 0.126 MAPE against 25.234/0.171 uncorrected and
20.479/0.134 for the strongest pinned object, with median paired reductions of
17.77\% MAE, 15.95\% RMSE, and 20.14\% MAPE. On EPF it averages 0.349/0.290
against 0.376/0.315 and 0.362/0.303, reducing the median MSE by 4.57\% and MAE by
5.44\%. The published operators are close to inactive in this regime, with median
PEMS reductions of 0.00\% RMSE and 0.36\%/0.31\% MAPE, because multiscale input
matching returns neighbourhoods dominated by the traffic profile the backbone
already predicts.

\paragraph{Improvement is broad, not only on average.}
An average can hide a correction that helps some settings and hurts others, so we
also count settings in which \emph{every} reported metric decreases, which \method{}
achieves in 541 of 585: 326/364 long-term, 156/156 PEMS, and 59/65 EPF, against 490
and 478 for the two pinned objects and 384 and 375 for \saraf{} and \raft{}, which
lose mostly by having nothing to contribute, returning a forecast identical to the
base in 121 and 131 settings against 50 for \residualknn{} and 3 for \method{}.
Compared setting by setting, \method{} improves every metric relative to
\analogknn{} in 453 settings against 54 the other way, and relative to
\residualknn{} in 457 against 63. On the five most accurate backbones the counts are
190/225 against 170, 157, 80, and 77.

\paragraph{Correcting 13 end-to-end trained forecasters.}
The comparison above holds base forecasts fixed to isolate the retrieval object. On
the separate primary matrix, where each backbone is trained and corrected end to
end, the error-only form of \method{}---Equation~\ref{eq:dial} at $\beta\equiv0$
with single-window selection---improves every reported metric in 562/585 settings
(96.1\%): 355/364 long-term, 151/156 PEMS, and 56/65 EPF, with mean paired
reductions of 8.5\% MSE, 7.5\% MAE, 10.6\% RMSE, and 12.0\% MAPE. Gains scale with
how much error there is to remove, from 1.1\% long-term MSE for \timexer{} to 41.3\%
for \informer{}, as an additive layer should, and no backbone is left behind
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:backbone-results}). The selection rule was fixed on a
predetermined 123-setting development subset, and on the 462 held-out settings the
strict-win rate is 437/462 (Appendix~\ref{app:aggregate-results}). A locked
correction also survives the passage of time: replaying \timemixer{} at horizon 96
on a timeline beginning after the standard test split, with labels released only as
their targets elapse, improves both metrics on all four ETT datasets and three seeds
(Appendix~\ref{app:ett}).

\paragraph{Ensembles, AutoML, and foundation models.}
Because \method{} consumes only predictions and targets, the same interface corrects
systems that are not single models. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:strong-systems} reports
81/94 strict wins over greedy forward selection, a portfolio and a zero-shot
ensemble, \autogluon{}'s \texttt{high\_quality} predictor, and \chronosbolt{} in
fine-tuned and zero-shot form
\citep{caruana2004ensemble,autosklearn_portfolio_zeroshot,shchur2023autogluon,
ansari2024chronos}, from 12/16 to 16/16 per system, with the retrieval family
selected in 70 settings and 65 of those strict wins: multi-component systems and an
off-distribution foundation model still leave error structure their own machinery
does not remove.

\subsection{Further Analysis}
\label{sec:exp-analysis}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/retrieval_object_vs_strength.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The better retrieval object depends on how good the underlying
  forecaster already is.} \residualknn{} minus \analogknn{} median worst-metric gain
  (percentage points, symmetric-log axis) per backbone, ordered from most to least
  accurate by long-term base MSE.}
  \label{fig:object-strength}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Which object is better depends on how strong the forecaster is.}
Ordering the 13 backbones by long-term base MSE, the advantage of retrieving errors
over retrieving futures falls as base error rises in both task families, with
Spearman $\rho$ of $-0.94$ and $-0.77$ and a sign change at \timesnet{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:object-strength}). The mechanism is the drift term of
Equation~\ref{eq:objects}: a weak forecaster's prediction holds little level,
phase, or rhythm worth keeping, so replacing it with an analogous future is an
improvement, whereas an accurate forecaster's prediction already holds all of that
and only its residual is worth transferring. \informer{}, whose long-term base MSE
is three times the best backbone's, is what pulls the ETTh2 and ETTm2 averages
towards \analogknn{}.

\paragraph{The selected object follows the forecast horizon.}
Validation puts non-zero weight on the drift term in 435 of 585 settings, and where
it does declines with the horizon: 50/52, 50/52, and 112/117 settings at horizons 6,
12, and 24, then 69/91, 65/91, 64/91, and 25/91 at 96 through 720. A neighbour's
trajectory stays usable for a few hours of traffic flow and is least usable a month
ahead, where the weight survives in barely a quarter of settings. It is also rarely
extreme, as 299 of those 435 settings use $\beta\in\{0.25,0.5\}$ rather than the full
overwrite input retrieval performs, so the selected operating point is one neither
published object can express. Retrieval of either kind is selected in 449 of the 585
settings, taking all 156 PEMS and 63 of 65 EPF settings and 230 of 364 long-term
ones, where a seasonal blend takes 75 and an overlap blend 46 (Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:selection}).

\paragraph{What the two-fold score and the free dial each contribute.}
Scoring candidates on the worse of the two chronological validation halves instead of
the whole window raises strict wins from 533 to 552 with the dial pinned shut.
Opening the dial then trades 11 of those wins for magnitude everywhere: the median
worst-metric gain rises from 9.73\% to 15.69\% on PEMS, from 1.77\% to 3.56\% on EPF,
and from 3.64\% to 4.17\% in long-term MSE. Restricting $\beta>0$ to horizons of at
most 24 steps unless it clears the best $\beta=0$ candidate by $0.05$ recovers those
11 wins, reaching 555/585, but gives back the long-term magnitude (median MSE 3.64\%
again). We report the free dial, because the choice between per-setting accuracy and
per-setting win rate is a deployment preference rather than a property of the
method.

\paragraph{Where the correction layer fails.}
Of the 585 settings, 44 are not strict wins: 38 long-term and 6 EPF, none on PEMS,
concentrated on ETTm1 (17), ETTh1 (8), ETTh2 (7), and EPF-FR (6), and on the most
accurate backbones, where the base trajectory is the thing worth protecting. Only 7
degrade every metric and 3 leave the forecast untouched; the other 34 trade one
metric for another, the largest single loss being 6.9\% MSE on an EPF-FR setting that
gains 16.4\% MAE. Gains can also be thin, with the worst metric improving by at most
one point in 68 of the 541 strict wins, and selecting on a single validation window
fails harder: 15 of its 52 non-strict settings degrade every metric.
