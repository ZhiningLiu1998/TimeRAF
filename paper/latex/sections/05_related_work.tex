\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Time-series forecasting.}
Classical models encode trend and seasonality
explicitly~\citep{anderson1976arima}, while neural forecasters attack the same
structure with frequency decomposition, patching, cross-variable attention,
multiscale mixing, exogenous conditioning, linear baselines, or large-corpus
pretraining \citep{zhou2022fedformer,wu2023timesnet,patchtst,tan2024pattn,
liu2024itransformer,wang2024timemixer,wang2024timexer,dlinear,lightts,
yi2024frets,ansari2024chronos}. All improve the forecast by changing the
forecaster; we show that even the strongest of them leaves a recurring,
retrievable error behind.

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented forecasting.}
Retrieval has supplied cross-entity context~\citep{yang2022mqretnn}, guided a
diffusion sampler~\citep{liu2024ratd}, populated LLM prompts~\citep{yang2024timerag},
and conditioned foundation models~\citep{tire2024raf}. \citet{zhang2024timeraf} train a retriever with
channel prompting under the same acronym as our method; \tsrag{} adds an encoder
and adaptive mixer~\citep{ning2025tsrag}; \raft{} targets recurring patterns model
weights miss~\citep{han2025raft}; and \crossrag{}, \craftm{}, and \saraf{} sharpen
relevance through cross-attention, spectral ranking, and stationarity-aware
diversity~\citep{lee2026crossrag,kang2026craft,zhou2026saraf}. All retrieve on the
input and inject trajectories or latents into a learned prediction path. We change
the retrieval object instead, and show it is one end of a dial their formulation
fixes.

\paragraph{Forecast combination and post-hoc correction.}
Ensembles improve robustness by combining predictors
\citep{kourentzes2014operator,oliveira2015ensemblebagging,mienye2022ensemble,
caruana2004ensemble,autosklearn_portfolio_zeroshot}, \autogluon{} automates
selection, stacking, and weighting~\citep{shchur2023autogluon}, and \timefuse{}
learns sample-dependent fusion weights~\citep{liu2025timefuse}. These recombine
forecasts; \method{} composes with them, asking whether the forecast a system
produces has a recurring retrievable error---which, in 70 of 94 such settings, it
does. Bias correction and residual boosting are classical special cases of our
candidate set, and their presence makes the retrieval claim falsifiable.
